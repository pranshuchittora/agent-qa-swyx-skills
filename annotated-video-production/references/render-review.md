# Final-render review

## Evidence and scope

Use samples from the final encoded deliverable at every cue, not images recomposited independently from the renderer. Start/settled and near-end frames are an efficient first pass. Add frames at camera cuts. Play each moving insert to check its useful action and timing; listen to representative and problematic audio transitions. State which checks were actually completed.

Build a chronological review board with cue ID/time, planned visual, current output, original asset, findings, proposed correction, and keep/refine/replace/remove disposition. Keep links to the complete source and final video time. Do not put internal research notes on the viewer-facing cards.

## Questions for each cue

1. Is this the selected visual, rather than a text fallback or adjacent product?
2. Does it add something the viewer cannot get from the speech alone?
3. Can the useful detail be read at playback size before the insert disappears?
4. Is the active speaker's face clear throughout camera changes?
5. Do highlights fit whole lines and remain aligned during motion?
6. Does the demo show the action/result, rather than title animation, presenter intro, or teaser?
7. Is attribution concise and useful? Is the URL readable without taking over the layout?
8. Is the repeated treatment becoming monotonous? Would silence and the speaker alone be better here?
9. Does the sound cue support the entrance without masking speech? Are the visual and sound timings aligned?

## Lessons from the High Taste Testers audit

These are examples of concrete failures, not a requirement that every future video use the same assets or cue count.

- **Muse Charm:** the saved post screenshot had a blank image region. A video downloader returned “No video could be found”; the full render replaced the promised product photo with a text excerpt. The photo was recoverable from the post's photo viewer. Check the media type and visually verify the attachment.
- **Asset-to-render drift:** 34 of 59 inserts in the audited render were text-only. Some were intentional pull quotes; others replaced available source visuals. The existence of a source manifest did not prevent the renderer's hard-coded quote map from bypassing it.
- **Useful source visuals omitted:** the Executor hub diagram, Link payment example, Poke phone screen, Grok Bot product UI, and Devin computer-use section had local captures but became slogans. Check actual media inputs against the selected visual, not just source URLs.
- **Capture quality:** the Clef post capture contained spinners; the Grok cost capture did not contain the complete intended chart. An attribution or generic quote does not repair those captures.
- **Disclaimers:** “Not a measured benchmark” on a qualitative discussion card and “Not the guest's private workspace” on a product card distracted from the conversation. Retain source distinctions internally; choose an honest asset and simple caption.
- **Detail too small:** terminal demos, dense process diagrams, benchmark charts, and the Raycast UI were technically present but hard to read. Crop to the useful action or detail, enlarge it, and provide enough reading time.
- **Bad clip choice:** a challenge clip reached “COMING UP”; a CLI clip spent the sampled opening on branding. Download success did not establish a useful segment.
- **Repeated material:** the same challenge clip returned later without showing a different useful moment. A repeated topic does not automatically need the same insert again.
- **Player chrome:** a final talk insert showed a play button and controls. Use the clean source thumbnail or a meaningful moving excerpt.
- **Framing:** a large hardware photo overlapped the speaker region while the actual hardware remained relatively small inside the photo. Crop the source and protect the speaker; simply scaling the whole image is not enough.

## Completion language

Say “inspected all cues at two sampled points” when that is what happened. Do not call it a full playback or audio review. Source recapture, revised plans, and new artwork are not a corrected render. A corrected local render is not a changed live upload.
